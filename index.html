TL;DR FoundSurface reconstructs 3D scene surfaces from unposed images in a single feed-forward pass. Rays are cast at a novel view and the network predicts how far each one travels, giving a compact ray-distance field from which the surface follows directly — no camera poses, no per-scene optimisation, no meshing.

01 — INPUT

Truly unposed

Raw images straight from a phone or a scan. No camera poses, no calibration, no SfM preprocessing.

02 — REPRESENTATION

Rays, not depth

Every pixel is a ray: its predicted distance gives the surface point, and normals follow in closed form — one analytic derivative, no normal head, no depth back-projection.

03 — OUTPUT

One pass, full resolution

Consistent surfaces at full image resolution from a single forward pass — no per-scene optimisation, no meshing.

How it works

One ray per pixel. Cast it, find where it stops, and the travelled length is the pixel value.

STEP 01

Unposed images in

Raw photos go in. The network recovers the scene and the cameras in the same pass — nothing to calibrate, nothing to pre-process.

STEP 02

Cast the rays

For the view we want, every pixel defines a ray. The network predicts where each ray meets the surface, giving a first, coarse estimate.

STEP 03

Raylet refinement

A light second stage sharpens that estimate, so edges and thin structures stay crisp.

Beyond depth. A depth map only records distance along the camera axis; FoundSurface stores the full length of every ray, so the surface follows directly — no back-projection step.

Results

Abstract

Reconstructing 3D scene surfaces from unposed images is challenging because it couples geometry, camera estimation and rendering. We present FoundSurface, a feed-forward approach that represents a scene by ray distance: for every pixel of a target view, the distance that its ray travels before reaching the surface. Starting from unposed images, a feed-forward transformer reconstructs scene tokens and cameras; a ray map for the target view is then fused with these tokens to predict a coarse distance field, which a second stage refines with sparse surface points. The ray-based formulation avoids depth back-projection and per-scene optimisation while keeping the representation compact, and yields full-resolution ray-distance maps from which consistent surfaces can be recovered.
